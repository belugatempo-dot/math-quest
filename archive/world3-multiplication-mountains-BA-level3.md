## World Overview

### Theme: Mountain Adventure
Young mathematicians embark on an epic journey through the Multiplication Mountains, a mystical range where numbers hold ancient secrets. Each peak presents new challenges—from the geometric puzzles of Shape Summit to the mysterious equations of Variable Valley. The ultimate goal: reach the highest peak, Fraction Falls, where the legendary Math Crystal awaits.

### Visual Design
- **Primary Colors**: Rocky grays, adventure oranges, snow whites, sky blues
- **Environment**: Mountain trails, caves, alpine meadows, crystal caverns
- **Characters**: Mountain guide (Sage Multiplico), geometric creatures, fraction sprites

### Narrative Arc
The Math Kingdom faces a mystery: the four ancient Math Crystals have been scattered across the Multiplication Mountains. Only a mathematician who masters shapes, multiplication, variables, and fractions can reunite them and restore balance to the realm.

## Unit A: Shape Summit (Weeks 1-9)
### Book 3A Topics: Shapes, Skip-Counting, Perimeter, Area

**Story Introduction**: The first Math Crystal lies hidden in Shape Summit, guarded by the Guardian of Geometry. To claim it, adventurers must prove their understanding of triangles, quadrilaterals, and the art of counting by groups.

---

### Chapter 1: Triangle Trails (Weeks 1-2)
**Topics**: Triangles, Quadrilaterals, Polygons, Polyominoes  
**BA Syllabus Weeks**: 1-3  
**Narrative**: Begin the ascent on Triangle Trails, where the path itself is made of triangular stepping stones.

#### Level 1-1: The Three-Sided Path
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Introduction to triangles — 3 sides, 3 corners (vertices)  
**Story**: Sage Multiplico explains that all paths in the mountains have shapes with meaning.  
**Problem**:  
> "Welcome, young explorer! This path is made of triangles. Count the sides of this shape. How many sides does a triangle have?"  
**Answer**: 3  
**Teaching Point**: A triangle always has exactly 3 sides and 3 vertices.

#### Level 1-2: Classifying Triangles
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Equilateral, isosceles, and scalene triangles  
**Problem**:  
> "Look at these three triangles on the mountain wall. Which one has all sides the same length?"  
> Triangle A: sides 5, 5, 5  
> Triangle B: sides 5, 5, 3  
> Triangle C: sides 3, 4, 5  
**Answer**: Triangle A  
**Teaching Point**: Equilateral = all sides equal, Isosceles = two sides equal, Scalene = no sides equal.

#### Level 1-3: Triangle Side Challenge
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Applying triangle classification  
**Problem**:  
> "The mountain bridge has an isosceles triangle shape. Two sides are 9 units and one side is 4 units. What are all the side lengths?"  
**Answer**: 9, 9, 4  
**Hint 1**: "What does 'isosceles' mean?"  
**Hint 2**: "An isosceles triangle has exactly two sides that are equal."

#### Level 1-4: The Quadrilateral Crossing
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Introduction to quadrilaterals — 4 sides  
**Story**: The path crosses a bridge shaped like a four-sided figure.  
**Problem**:  
> "This bridge has 4 sides. What do we call a shape with 4 sides?"  
**Answer**: Quadrilateral  
**Teaching Point**: "Quad" means 4, "lateral" refers to sides.

#### Level 1-5: Special Quadrilaterals
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Rectangles, squares, parallelograms, trapezoids  
**Problem**:  
> "Match each quadrilateral to its description:  
> A. Square  
> B. Rectangle  
> C. Parallelogram  
> Which one has 4 equal sides AND 4 right angles?"  
**Answer**: A (Square)

#### Level 1-6: Polygon Peaks
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Polygons with more than 4 sides  
**Problem**:  
> "The summit marker is a shape with 5 sides. What is this polygon called?"  
**Answer**: Pentagon  
**Teaching Point**: Pentagon (5), Hexagon (6), Heptagon (7), Octagon (8)

#### Level 1-7: Count the Sides
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Identifying polygons by side count  
**Problem**:  
> "How many sides does a hexagon have? And an octagon?"  
**Answer**: Hexagon = 6, Octagon = 8

#### Level 1-8: Polyomino Puzzle
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Introduction to polyominoes — shapes made from unit squares  
**Story**: Crystal fragments in the cave are shaped like connected squares.  
**Problem**:  
> "A tetromino is made of 4 unit squares. How many unit squares make up a pentomino?"  
**Answer**: 5  
**Teaching Point**: Polyominoes are named by their number of squares (domino = 2, tromino = 3, tetromino = 4, pentomino = 5).

#### Level 1-9: Build the Shape ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Polyomino construction and area  
**Problem**:  
> "Using exactly 5 unit squares connected edge-to-edge, draw a pentomino that fits inside a 3×3 grid. How many different pentominoes can you make?"  
**Answer**: There are 12 distinct pentominoes (not counting rotations/reflections as different). Students need to draw any valid pentomino.  
**Hint**: "Start with a line of 3 squares, then add 2 more."

### Chapter 2: Skip-Count Caverns (Weeks 3-4)
**Topics**: Skip-Counting, Hundreds Chart, Balancing  
**BA Syllabus Weeks**: 4-5  
**Narrative**: Deep inside Skip-Count Caverns, treasures are hidden at regular intervals. Master the art of counting by groups to find them!

#### Level 2-1: Counting by 2s
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Skip-counting foundation  
**Problem**:  
> "The cave crystals glow every 2 steps. Starting at 2, what are the next 5 numbers you'll see glowing crystals?"  
**Answer**: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10  
**Teaching Point**: Skip-counting is repeated addition: 2, 2+2, 2+2+2...

#### Level 2-2: Counting by 5s
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Skip-counting by 5s  
**Problem**:  
> "Count by 5s from 5 to 50. Circle all the numbers that end in 0."  
**Answer**: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50 (circled: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50)

#### Level 2-3: Counting by Different Numbers
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Skip-counting by 3s, 4s, 6s  
**Problem**:  
> "Skip-count by 4s starting at 4. What is the 7th number in your count?"  
**Answer**: 28 (4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28)

#### Level 2-4: The Hundreds Chart
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Using the hundreds chart for patterns  
**Story**: The cave wall shows an ancient hundreds chart.  
**Problem**:  
> "On the hundreds chart, start at 7. Add 10. Where do you land? Add 10 again. What pattern do you notice?"  
**Answer**: 7 → 17 → 27. Pattern: The ones digit stays the same, the tens digit increases by 1.

#### Level 2-5: Chart Patterns
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Identifying patterns on hundreds chart  
**Problem**:  
> "What do all numbers in this sequence have in common: 3, 13, 23, 33, 43?"  
**Answer**: They all have 3 in the ones place.

#### Level 2-6: Skip-Count Intersection ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Finding common multiples through skip-counting  
**Problem** (from BA3 Syllabus):  
> "Ben skip-counts by 6s starting at 6. Jen skip-counts by 9s starting at 9. What is the largest two-digit number that Ben and Jen both say?"  
**Answer**: 90  
**Hint 1**: "List Ben's numbers: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48, 54, 60, 66, 72, 78, 84, 90, 96..."  
**Hint 2**: "List Jen's numbers: 9, 18, 27, 36, 45, 54, 63, 72, 81, 90, 99..."  
**Solution**: Common numbers include 18, 36, 54, 72, 90. The largest two-digit one is 90.

#### Level 2-7: Balance Scale Introduction
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Balance and equality  
**Problem**:  
> "A balance scale has 15 on the left side. What must go on the right side to balance?"  
**Answer**: 15  
**Teaching Point**: When a scale balances, both sides are equal.

#### Level 2-8: Missing Number Balance
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Finding missing values for balance  
**Problem**:  
> "The left side has: 20 + 10. The right side has: 15 + ___. What goes in the blank to balance?"  
**Answer**: 15 (because 20 + 10 = 30, and 30 = 15 + 15)

#### Level 2-9: Multi-Step Balance ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Complex balancing problems  
**Problem**:  
> "If 3 triangles balance with 12, how many triangles balance with 20?"  
**Answer**: 5 (each triangle = 4, so 20 ÷ 4 = 5)  
**Hint**: "First find what one triangle equals."

### Chapter 3: Perimeter & Area Pass (Weeks 5-6)
**Topics**: Perimeter, Area  
**BA Syllabus Weeks**: 6-8  
**Narrative**: To cross Perimeter Pass and reach Area Plateau, adventurers must measure the mountain's mysterious shapes.

#### Level 3-1: What is Perimeter?
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Perimeter as the distance around  
**Problem**:  
> "You walk around a triangular meadow. The sides are 5 m, 7 m, and 8 m. How far did you walk in total?"  
**Answer**: 20 m  
**Teaching Point**: Perimeter = sum of all side lengths.

#### Level 3-2: Rectangle Perimeter
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Perimeter formula for rectangles  
**Problem**:  
> "A rectangular cabin has length 10 ft and width 6 ft. What is its perimeter?"  
**Answer**: 32 ft (10 + 6 + 10 + 6 = 32)

#### Level 3-3: Isosceles Triangle Perimeter ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Perimeter with constraints  
**Problem** (from BA3 Syllabus):  
> "What is the perimeter of an isosceles triangle with sides of length 9 and 4?"  
**Answer**: There are TWO possible answers: 22 (if sides are 9, 9, 4) or 17 (if sides are 4, 4, 9)  
**Teaching Point**: This problem has two valid interpretations! Both 9+9+4=22 and 4+4+9=17 are correct. (Note: Actually 4+4+9=17 wouldn't form a valid triangle since 4+4=8<9. So only 22 works.)  
**Correct Answer**: 22 (only 9, 9, 4 forms a valid triangle)  
**Hint**: "In a triangle, the sum of any two sides must be greater than the third side."

#### Level 3-4: Find the Missing Side
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Working backwards from perimeter  
**Problem**:  
> "A square has perimeter 36 cm. What is the length of one side?"  
**Answer**: 9 cm (36 ÷ 4 = 9)

#### Level 3-5: What is Area?
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Area as the space inside  
**Story**: The mountain meadow is divided into unit squares.  
**Problem**:  
> "Count the unit squares that cover this rectangle: [3 × 4 grid shown]. What is the area?"  
**Answer**: 12 square units

#### Level 3-6: Rectangle Area
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Area formula (length × width)  
**Problem**:  
> "A cabin floor is 7 meters long and 5 meters wide. What is the area of the floor?"  
**Answer**: 35 square meters  
**Teaching Point**: Area of rectangle = length × width

#### Level 3-7: Area vs Perimeter
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Distinguishing area and perimeter  
**Problem**:  
> "A rectangle is 6 units by 3 units. Calculate BOTH its perimeter AND its area."  
**Answer**: Perimeter = 18 units, Area = 18 square units  
**Teaching Point**: Different measurements can have the same number but different units!

#### Level 3-8: Complex Area ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Finding area of composite shapes  
**Problem** (based on BA3 Syllabus):  
> "A rectangle with width 21 ft and height 16 ft is split into six congruent rectangles arranged in a 2×3 pattern. What is the area of one small rectangle?"  
**Answer**: 56 sq ft (Total area = 21 × 16 = 336, divided by 6 = 56)  
**Hint 1**: "Find the total area first."  
**Hint 2**: "If 6 rectangles are congruent (same size), divide total area by 6."

#### Level 3-9: Area Puzzle ⭐
**Type**: Boss Battle  
**Concept**: Combined perimeter and area reasoning  
**Problem**:  
> "A rectangle has a perimeter of 20 units. The length and width are whole numbers. What dimensions give the LARGEST possible area?"  
**Answer**: 5 × 5 = 25 square units (a square!)  
**Options to test**: 1×9=9, 2×8=16, 3×7=21, 4×6=24, 5×5=25  
**No hints available.**

### Book 3B Topics: Multiplication, Perfect Squares, Distributive Property

**Story Introduction**: The second Math Crystal is hidden in Multiplication Mesa, where the ancient Order of Multipliers once trained. Their secrets—commutative laws, perfect squares, and the powerful Distributive Property—must be mastered to proceed.

---

### Chapter 4: Multiplication Foundations (Weeks 10-12)
**Topics**: Multiplication Facts, Commutativity, Associativity  
**BA Syllabus Weeks**: 10-12  
**Narrative**: The training grounds of Multiplication Mesa begin with the fundamental arts of multiplication.

#### Level 4-1: What is Multiplication?
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Multiplication as repeated addition  
**Problem**:  
> "There are 4 tents at base camp. Each tent has 3 climbers. How many climbers in total? Write this as both addition and multiplication."  
**Answer**: 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12, or 4 × 3 = 12  
**Teaching Point**: Multiplication is a shortcut for repeated addition.

#### Level 4-2: Arrays
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Multiplication as arrays  
**Problem**:  
> "The supply crates are arranged in 5 rows with 6 crates in each row. How many crates total?"  
**Answer**: 30  
**Teaching Point**: An array shows multiplication visually. Rows × columns = total.

#### Level 4-3: Times Tables Practice
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Building multiplication fluency  
**Problem**:  
> "Solve: 7 × 8 = ___"  
**Answer**: 56  
**Hint**: "Think of 7 × 8 as (7 × 4) + (7 × 4) = 28 + 28"

#### Level 4-4: The Commutative Property
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Order doesn't matter in multiplication  
**Problem**:  
> "Is 6 × 4 the same as 4 × 6? Show why using arrays."  
**Answer**: Yes, both equal 24. A 6×4 array has the same number of squares as a 4×6 array.  
**Teaching Point**: a × b = b × a (The Commutative Property)

#### Level 4-5: Using Commutativity
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Applying commutative property  
**Problem**:  
> "Which is easier to compute: 8 × 5 or 5 × 8? Explain why and solve."  
**Answer**: Both equal 40. Many find 5 × 8 easier because multiplying by 5 follows a simple pattern (ends in 0 or 5).

#### Level 4-6: The Associative Property
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Grouping doesn't matter  
**Problem**:  
> "Calculate (2 × 3) × 4 and 2 × (3 × 4). What do you notice?"  
**Answer**: Both equal 24. (2 × 3) × 4 = 6 × 4 = 24, and 2 × (3 × 4) = 2 × 12 = 24  
**Teaching Point**: (a × b) × c = a × (b × c) (The Associative Property)

#### Level 4-7: Strategic Grouping
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Using associativity to simplify  
**Problem**:  
> "Calculate 5 × 7 × 2 by grouping the easy numbers first."  
**Answer**: 70. Group as 5 × 2 × 7 = 10 × 7 = 70

#### Level 4-8: Multiplication Puzzles ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Problem-solving with multiplication  
**Problem**:  
> "Fill in the blanks: ___ × ___ = 36, where both numbers are greater than 1 and less than 10. Find ALL solutions."  
**Answer**: 4 × 9, 6 × 6, 9 × 4 (also 2 × 18 and 3 × 12, but those have numbers outside 1-10)  
**Note**: Only 4×9, 6×6 work with the constraints.

#### Level 4-9: Three-Number Products ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Multi-step multiplication  
**Problem**:  
> "What is 5 × 4 × 5?"  
**Answer**: 100  
**Hint**: "Group strategically: (5 × 5) × 4 = 25 × 4"

### Chapter 5: Perfect Squares (Weeks 13-14)
**Topics**: Perfect Squares, Square Numbers, Patterns  
**BA Syllabus Weeks**: 13-14  
**Narrative**: The Perfect Square Plaza, where ancient mathematicians discovered the beauty of numbers multiplied by themselves.

#### Level 5-1: What is a Square Number?
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Introduction to perfect squares  
**Problem**:  
> "When you multiply a number by itself, you get a square number. What is 4 × 4?"  
**Answer**: 16  
**Teaching Point**: 16 is a "perfect square" because 4 × 4 = 16. We write this as 4² = 16.

#### Level 5-2: Square Arrays
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Visualizing squares  
**Problem**:  
> "Draw an array that shows 5 × 5. What shape is it? How many total squares?"  
**Answer**: 25 total squares, arranged in a square shape (5 rows, 5 columns)

#### Level 5-3: First Ten Squares
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Memorizing key squares  
**Problem**:  
> "Complete this list of perfect squares: 1² = 1, 2² = 4, 3² = 9, 4² = ___, 5² = ___"  
**Answer**: 4² = 16, 5² = 25

#### Level 5-4: Recognizing Squares
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Identifying perfect squares  
**Problem**:  
> "Which of these numbers are perfect squares? 20, 25, 30, 36, 40, 49"  
**Answer**: 25 (5²), 36 (6²), 49 (7²)

#### Level 5-5: Square Patterns
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Patterns in perfect squares  
**Problem**:  
> "Look at the differences between consecutive squares: 4-1=3, 9-4=5, 16-9=7, 25-16=9. What's the pattern? What is 36-25?"  
**Answer**: 36-25 = 11. Pattern: differences are consecutive odd numbers (3, 5, 7, 9, 11...)  
**Teaching Point**: The difference between consecutive squares is always an odd number!

#### Level 5-6: Building Squares
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Constructing squares from patterns  
**Problem**:  
> "If 7² = 49, use the odd number pattern to find 8²."  
**Answer**: 8² = 49 + 15 = 64 (the next odd number after 13 is 15)

#### Level 5-7: Area and Squares
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Connecting squares to area  
**Problem**:  
> "A square garden has sides of 9 feet. What is the area?"  
**Answer**: 81 square feet (9² = 81)

#### Level 5-8: Sums of Squares ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Working with multiple squares  
**Problem**:  
> "What is 3² + 4²?"  
**Answer**: 9 + 16 = 25  
**Bonus**: Notice that 25 = 5²! (This is related to the 3-4-5 right triangle.)

#### Level 5-9: Find the Square ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Reverse engineering squares  
**Problem**:  
> "A number times itself equals 64. What is that number?"  
**Answer**: 8

### Chapter 6: Distributive Property & Order of Operations (Weeks 15-17)
**Topics**: Order of Operations, Area Model, Distributive Property  
**BA Syllabus Weeks**: 15-17  
**Narrative**: The ancient Order of Operations carved their rules into the mountain stones. Master these, along with the powerful Distributive Property, to break apart difficult problems!

#### Level 6-1: Order of Operations Introduction
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Why order matters  
**Problem**:  
> "Two climbers solve 3 + 4 × 2 differently. Alex gets 14, Bailey gets 11. Who is correct and why?"  
**Answer**: Bailey is correct (11). Multiplication comes before addition: 4 × 2 = 8, then 3 + 8 = 11.  
**Teaching Point**: Order of Operations: Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication/Division (left to right), Addition/Subtraction (left to right). "PEMDAS"

#### Level 6-2: Parentheses First
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Parentheses override order  
**Problem**:  
> "Calculate (3 + 4) × 2"  
**Answer**: 14. Parentheses first: 3 + 4 = 7, then 7 × 2 = 14.  
**Teaching Point**: Parentheses are like saying "do this first!"

#### Level 6-3: Practice Order of Operations
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Applying PEMDAS  
**Problem**:  
> "Solve: 20 - 3 × 4 + 2"  
**Answer**: 10. First 3×4=12, then 20-12+2=10.

#### Level 6-4: Area Model Introduction
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Breaking apart multiplication visually  
**Problem**:  
> "To find 7 × 12, break 12 into 10 + 2. Draw an area model and solve."  
**Answer**: 7 × 12 = 7 × (10 + 2) = 7×10 + 7×2 = 70 + 14 = 84

#### Level 6-5: The Distributive Property
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Formal introduction  
**Story**: "The ancient Distributive Property lets you 'distribute' multiplication across addition!"  
**Problem**:  
> "Use the distributive property to calculate 6 × 15 by breaking 15 into 10 + 5."  
**Answer**: 6 × 15 = 6 × (10 + 5) = 6×10 + 6×5 = 60 + 30 = 90  
**Teaching Point**: a × (b + c) = (a × b) + (a × c)

#### Level 6-6: Breaking Apart Factors
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Strategic use of distributive property  
**Problem**:  
> "Calculate 8 × 99 using the distributive property."  
**Answer**: 8 × 99 = 8 × (100 - 1) = 800 - 8 = 792  
**Hint**: "What's easier: 8 × 99 or 8 × 100 minus something?"

#### Level 6-7: Area Model Puzzles
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Working with area models  
**Problem**:  
> "An area model shows a rectangle split into two parts: 5 × 20 and 5 × 3. What single multiplication does this represent?"  
**Answer**: 5 × 23 = 115

#### Level 6-8: Complex Expressions ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Multi-step order of operations  
**Problem**:  
> "Solve: 4 × (10 - 3) + 5²"  
**Answer**: 4 × 7 + 25 = 28 + 25 = 53

#### Level 6-9: Distribution Puzzle ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Advanced distributive property  
**Problem**:  
> "Find the value of 25 × 17 + 25 × 3 without multiplying directly."  
**Answer**: 25 × (17 + 3) = 25 × 20 = 500  
**Teaching Point**: The distributive property works in reverse too! (Factoring)

## Unit C: Variable Valley (Weeks 19-27)
### Book 3C Topics: Variables, Equations, Division, Remainders

**Story Introduction**: The third Math Crystal lies in Variable Valley, where the mysterious Variables—letters that stand for unknown numbers—hold the key. To claim it, adventurers must also master Division and the curious Remainders that sometimes appear.

---

### Chapter 7: Variables & Equations (Weeks 19-21)
**Topics**: Variables, Writing Equations, Solving Equations  
**BA Syllabus Weeks**: 19-21  
**Narrative**: In Variable Valley, unknowns are represented by symbols. Learn to write and solve equations to uncover hidden values!

#### Level 7-1: What is a Variable?
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Introduction to variables  
**Problem**:  
> "There are some apples in a basket. We don't know how many, so we call the number 'n'. If someone adds 5 more apples, how many apples are there now?"  
**Answer**: n + 5  
**Teaching Point**: A variable is a letter that represents an unknown number.

#### Level 7-2: Expressions with Variables
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Writing simple expressions  
**Problem**:  
> "Write an expression: 'A number x, multiplied by 3'"  
**Answer**: 3x or x × 3

#### Level 7-3: Evaluating Expressions
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Substituting values  
**Problem**:  
> "If n = 7, what is n + 12?"  
**Answer**: 19

#### Level 7-4: Writing Equations
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Equations represent balanced statements  
**Problem**:  
> "Write an equation: 'A number y plus 8 equals 15'"  
**Answer**: y + 8 = 15  
**Teaching Point**: An equation uses '=' to show two things are equal.

#### Level 7-5: Solving by Guess and Check
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Basic equation solving  
**Problem**:  
> "What value of x makes this true? x + 6 = 14"  
**Answer**: x = 8

#### Level 7-6: Inverse Operations
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Undoing operations to solve  
**Problem**:  
> "To solve n - 5 = 12, what operation undoes subtraction? Solve for n."  
**Answer**: Addition undoes subtraction. n = 12 + 5 = 17  
**Teaching Point**: Addition and subtraction are inverse operations. So are multiplication and division.

#### Level 7-7: Multiplication Equations
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Solving equations with multiplication  
**Problem** (based on BA style):  
> "If ▲ + ▲ + ▲ = 24, what is ▲?"  
**Answer**: ▲ = 8 (because 3 × ▲ = 24, so ▲ = 24 ÷ 3 = 8)

#### Level 7-8: Two-Step Equations ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Equations requiring multiple steps  
**Problem**:  
> "Solve: 2 × m + 4 = 20"  
**Answer**: m = 8  
**Solution**: 2m + 4 = 20 → 2m = 16 → m = 8

#### Level 7-9: Equation Word Problem ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Translating words to equations  
**Problem**:  
> "I think of a number, multiply it by 5, then subtract 3. The result is 22. What was my number?"  
**Answer**: 5 (5 × 5 - 3 = 22)  
**Hint**: "Write an equation: 5 × n - 3 = 22"

### Chapter 8: Division Basics (Weeks 22-24)
**Topics**: Division, Remainders  
**BA Syllabus Weeks**: 22-24  
**Narrative**: The Division Falls split the mountain stream into equal parts. Master division—and learn what happens when things don't divide evenly!

#### Level 8-1: What is Division?
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Division as sharing equally  
**Problem**:  
> "24 hikers need to split into 4 equal groups. How many hikers in each group?"  
**Answer**: 6  
**Teaching Point**: Division answers "How many in each group?" or "How many groups?"

#### Level 8-2: Division as Reverse Multiplication
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Relationship between × and ÷  
**Problem**:  
> "If 7 × 8 = 56, what is 56 ÷ 8?"  
**Answer**: 7  
**Teaching Point**: Division undoes multiplication. If a × b = c, then c ÷ b = a.

#### Level 8-3: Division Facts
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Building division fluency  
**Problem**:  
> "Solve: 63 ÷ 9 = ___"  
**Answer**: 7

#### Level 8-4: Division with Arrays
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Visualizing division  
**Problem**:  
> "45 dots are arranged in 5 equal rows. How many dots in each row? Write this as a division."  
**Answer**: 9 dots per row. 45 ÷ 5 = 9

#### Level 8-5: What are Remainders?
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: When division isn't exact  
**Problem**:  
> "14 hikers want to form groups of 3. How many complete groups? How many hikers are left over?"  
**Answer**: 4 groups with 2 left over (14 ÷ 3 = 4 R2)  
**Teaching Point**: The leftover is called the "remainder." We write 14 ÷ 3 = 4 R2.

#### Level 8-6: Finding Remainders
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Calculating remainders  
**Problem**:  
> "What is 23 ÷ 5?"  
**Answer**: 4 R3 (because 5 × 4 = 20, and 23 - 20 = 3)

#### Level 8-7: Remainder Patterns
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Understanding remainder constraints  
**Problem**:  
> "When dividing by 6, what are all the possible remainders?"  
**Answer**: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 (remainder is always less than the divisor)

#### Level 8-8: Remainder Word Problem ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Applying remainders in context  
**Problem**:  
> "A baker has 50 cookies. She puts 8 cookies in each box. How many full boxes can she make? How many cookies are left over?"  
**Answer**: 6 full boxes, 2 cookies left over (50 ÷ 8 = 6 R2)

#### Level 8-9: Chicken McNugget Problem ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: What numbers CAN'T be made? (Classic BA-style)  
**Problem** (from BA3 Syllabus):  
> "Fubert has only 3-gram and 7-gram weights. What is the largest weight that he CANNOT make by combining two or more of these weights?"  
**Answer**: 11 grams  
**Solution**: Can make: 3, 6, 7, 9, 10, 12, 13, 14... Cannot make: 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 11. Largest is 11.  
**Hint 1**: "List all numbers you CAN make using 3s and 7s."  
**Hint 2**: "3, 3+3=6, 7, 3+3+3=9, 3+7=10, 3+3+3+3=12..."

#### Level 9-1: Units of Length
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Inches, feet, yards, miles  
**Problem**:

**Answer**: 12 inches = 1 foot, 3 feet = 1 yard

#### Level 9-2: Converting Units
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Unit conversion  
**Problem**:  
> "Convert 36 inches to feet."

#### Level 9-3: Metric Units
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Centimeters, meters, kilometers

#### Level 9-4: Metric Conversions

#### Level 9-5: Weight/Mass Units

### Chapter 10: Introduction to Fractions (Weeks 28-31)
**Topics**: Unit Fractions, Types of Fractions, Parts of a Whole, Comparing  
**BA Syllabus Weeks**: 28-31  
**Narrative**: At Fraction Falls, water splits into equal streams. Each stream represents a fraction of the whole!

#### Level 10-1: What is a Fraction?
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Fractions as parts of a whole  
**Problem**:  
> "A pizza is cut into 8 equal slices. You eat 3 slices. What fraction of the pizza did you eat?"  
**Answer**: 3/8  
**Teaching Point**: A fraction has a numerator (parts you have) and denominator (total equal parts).

#### Level 10-2: Unit Fractions
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Fractions with numerator 1  
**Problem**:  
> "What is the unit fraction for 'one out of five equal parts'?"  
**Answer**: 1/5  
**Teaching Point**: Unit fractions have 1 as the numerator: 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5...

#### Level 10-3: Reading Fractions
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Identifying fractions from pictures  
**Problem**:  
> "A rectangle is divided into 6 equal parts. 4 parts are shaded. What fraction is shaded?"  
**Answer**: 4/6

#### Level 10-4: Whole-Number Fractions
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Fractions equal to whole numbers  
**Problem**:  
> "What whole number equals 12/4?"  
**Answer**: 3 (because 12 ÷ 4 = 3)  
**Teaching Point**: When the numerator is a multiple of the denominator, the fraction equals a whole number.

#### Level 10-5: Fractions Greater Than 1
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Improper fractions  
**Problem**:  
> "You have 7/4 of a pizza. Is this more or less than 1 whole pizza? How much more?"  
**Answer**: More than 1. It equals 1 and 3/4 pizzas (or 1¾).

#### Level 10-6: Mixed Numbers
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Converting between improper fractions and mixed numbers  
**Problem**:  
> "Write 11/3 as a mixed number."  
**Answer**: 3⅔ (because 11 ÷ 3 = 3 R2)

#### Level 10-7: Fractions on a Number Line
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Placing fractions on number lines  
**Problem**:  
> "Place 3/4 on a number line from 0 to 1. Is it closer to 0 or to 1?"  
**Answer**: Closer to 1 (3/4 = 0.75)

#### Level 10-8: Comparing Unit Fractions ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Counterintuitive comparison  
**Problem**:  
> "Which is larger: 1/3 or 1/5? Explain why."  
**Answer**: 1/3 is larger  
**Teaching Point**: With unit fractions, the SMALLER denominator means LARGER pieces!

#### Level 10-9: Comparing Fractions ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Same numerator or denominator  
**Problem**:  
> "Put in order from smallest to largest: 2/5, 2/3, 2/7"  
**Answer**: 2/7, 2/5, 2/3  
**Hint**: "When numerators are the same, compare denominators. Bigger denominator = smaller pieces."

---

### Chapter 11: Estimation & Rounding (Weeks 32-33)
**Topics**: Estimation, Rounding, Estimating Quotients  
**BA Syllabus Weeks**: 32-33  
**Narrative**: On Estimation Cliffs, exact answers aren't always possible—or necessary. Learn the art of smart approximation!

#### Level 11-1: What is Estimation?
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Approximation vs exact answers  
**Problem**:  
> "About how many days are in 3 months? (Don't calculate exactly!)"  
**Answer**: About 90 days (roughly 30 days per month)  
**Teaching Point**: Estimation gives a quick, reasonable answer without exact calculation.

#### Level 11-2: Rounding to Nearest 10
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Rounding basics  
**Problem**:  
> "Round 47 to the nearest 10."  
**Answer**: 50  
**Teaching Point**: Look at the ones digit. 5 or more rounds up, 4 or less rounds down.

#### Level 11-3: Rounding to Nearest 100
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Rounding larger numbers  
**Problem**:  
> "Round 849 to the nearest hundred."  
**Answer**: 800 (the tens digit is 4, so round down)

#### Level 11-4: Estimating Sums
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Using rounding to estimate  
**Problem**:  
> "Estimate 389 + 512 by rounding each number to the nearest hundred first."  
**Answer**: 400 + 500 = 900 (actual answer is 901)

#### Level 11-5: Estimating Products
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Estimation with multiplication  
**Problem**:  
> "Estimate 29 × 41 by rounding."  
**Answer**: 30 × 40 = 1,200 (actual is 1,189)

#### Level 11-6: Estimating Quotients
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Estimation with division  
**Problem**:  
> "Estimate 247 ÷ 5 without dividing exactly."  
**Answer**: About 50 (250 ÷ 5 = 50)  
**Teaching Point**: Round the dividend to a number that divides evenly.

#### Level 11-7: Is My Answer Reasonable?
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Using estimation to check work  
**Problem**:  
> "A student calculated 58 × 7 = 486. Is this reasonable? Estimate to check."  
**Answer**: Not reasonable. Estimate: 60 × 7 = 420, so 486 is too high. (Actual: 58 × 7 = 406)

#### Level 11-8: Fermi Estimation ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Real-world estimation  
**Problem**:  
> "About how many minutes are in one week? (Estimate without a calculator)"  
**Answer**: About 10,000 minutes  
**Solution**: 60 min/hr × 24 hr/day × 7 days ≈ 60 × 25 × 7 ≈ 60 × 175 ≈ 10,000 (actual: 10,080)

#### Level 11-9: Strategic Estimation ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Choosing rounding strategy  
**Problem**:  
> "You have $100. Books cost $13 each. Estimate: can you buy 8 books?"  
**Answer**: No. Estimate: $13 × 8 ≈ $15 × 8 = $120 > $100 (or $13 × 8 ≈ $10 × 8 = $80 is too low, actual is $104)

### Chapter 12: Area Measurement Mastery (Weeks 34-36)
**Topics**: Area Measurement, Area of Triangles, Final Review  
**BA Syllabus Weeks**: 34-35  
**Narrative**: The final climb! Master the measurement of complex areas, including the area of triangles, to reach the Crystal Peak!

#### Level 12-1: Area Units
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Square units for area  
**Problem**:  
> "Why do we measure area in 'square feet' or 'square meters' instead of just 'feet' or 'meters'?"  
**Answer**: Because area measures 2-dimensional space (length × width), which gives square units.

#### Level 12-2: Area of Rectangles Review
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Rectangle area formula  
**Problem**:  
> "Find the area of a rectangle with length 15 cm and width 8 cm."  
**Answer**: 120 square centimeters

#### Level 12-3: Area of Squares Review
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Square area  
**Problem**:  
> "A square garden has sides of 12 meters. What is its area?"  
**Answer**: 144 square meters (12² = 144)

#### Level 12-4: Triangle Area Introduction
**Type**: Teaching  
**Concept**: Area of a triangle  
**Problem**:  
> "A rectangle has area 24 square units. A triangle is made by cutting the rectangle diagonally in half. What is the triangle's area?"  
**Answer**: 12 square units (half of the rectangle)  
**Teaching Point**: Triangle area = ½ × base × height

#### Level 12-5: Triangle Area Formula
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Applying the formula  
**Problem**:  
> "Find the area of a triangle with base 10 cm and height 6 cm."  
**Answer**: 30 square cm (½ × 10 × 6 = 30)

#### Level 12-6: Finding Base or Height
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Working backwards  
**Problem**:  
> "A triangle has area 35 square units and base 7 units. What is the height?"  
**Answer**: 10 units (35 = ½ × 7 × h → 35 = 3.5h → h = 10)

#### Level 12-7: Composite Shapes
**Type**: Practice  
**Concept**: Adding areas  
**Problem**:  
> "An L-shaped figure is made of two rectangles: one is 5×3 and the other is 4×2. What is the total area?"  
**Answer**: 15 + 8 = 23 square units

#### Level 12-8: Subtracting Areas ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Finding area by subtraction  
**Problem**:  
> "A 10×10 square has a 3×3 square cut from one corner. What area remains?"  
**Answer**: 100 - 9 = 91 square units

#### Level 12-9: Final Area Challenge ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Concept**: Complex composite shape  
**Problem**:  
> "A house shape is made of a square (side 8) with a triangle (base 8, height 5) on top. What is the total area?"  
**Answer**: 64 + 20 = 84 square units  
**Solution**: Square: 8² = 64. Triangle: ½ × 8 × 5 = 20. Total: 84.
