## World Overview

The brave math explorers set sail for the legendary Fraction Islands—a mysterious archipelago where ancient civilizations encoded their wisdom in mathematical puzzles. ### Chapter 1: Angle Island (Weeks 1-3)
**Topics**: Shapes, Angles, Parallel & Perpendicular, Distributive Property  
**BA Syllabus Weeks**: 1-3  
**Narrative**: The temples of Angle Island hold secrets that can only be unlocked by understanding how angles work.

#### Level 4-1-1: The Temple Entrance
**Type**: Teaching  
**Category**: Thinking  
**Difficulty**: ⭐  
**Concept**: Angles as measures of rotation

**Story Context**:
> Captain Claw examines the temple door. "This door is stuck! See these two walls meeting? The angle between them is too tight for the door to swing open."

**Problem**:
> When two lines meet at a point, they form an angle. Look at the corner of this page. Is that angle bigger than, smaller than, or equal to the corner of a square?

**Answer**: Equal to (both are 90°, a right angle)

**Hints**:
- Hint 1 (free): "What do you know about the corners of a square?"
- Hint 2 (1⭐): "The corner of a page and the corner of a square both form 'right angles.'"
- Hint 3 (2⭐): "Right angles are exactly 90 degrees. Compare the page corner to the square corner."

**Teaching Point**: A right angle measures exactly 90°. Corners of rectangles and squares are right angles.

---

#### Level 4-1-2: Measuring Without Numbers
**Type**: Practice  
**Category**: Compare Without Calculating  
**Difficulty**: ⭐⭐

**Story Context**:
> Lizzie finds two carved symbols on the wall—two different angles. "We need to figure out which one is bigger WITHOUT measuring!"

**Problem**:
> Look at these two angles:  
> Angle A opens like a book halfway.  
> Angle B opens like a door fully swung.  
> Which angle is larger? Don't use a protractor!

**Answer**: Angle B (a fully open door ~180° vs. a book halfway ~90°)

**Hints**:
- Hint 1 (free): "Imagine actually opening a book and a door. Which opens wider?"
- Hint 2 (1⭐): "A book opened halfway makes about a right angle (90°)."
- Hint 3 (2⭐): "A door fully swung open is close to a straight line (180°)."

**Teaching Point**: We can compare angles by imagining real-world examples—no measurement needed!

#### Level 4-1-3: Angle Arithmetic
**Type**: Thinking  
**Category**: Multiple Solution Paths  
**Difficulty**: ⭐⭐

**Story Context**:
> "The temple lock needs two angles that add to 90°," Grogg reads. "I found a 35° angle. What's the other one?"

**Problem**:
> Two angles are called **complementary** if they add up to 90°. If one angle is 35°, what is its complement?

**Answer**: 55°

**Hints**:
- Hint 1 (free): "Complementary angles add to 90°. How would you find the missing piece?"
- Hint 2 (1⭐): "If one part is 35°, and the whole is 90°, what's left?"
- Hint 3 (2⭐): "Subtract the known angle from 90°. What's the complement?"

**Teaching Point**: Complementary angles sum to 90°. To find one, subtract the known angle from 90°.

#### Level 4-1-4: Supplementary Secrets
**Type**: Teaching  
**Category**: Thinking  
**Difficulty**: ⭐⭐

**Story Context**:
> Professor Owlbert appears on a carving. "Two angles on a straight line... what can you say about their sum?"

**Problem**:
> A straight line measures 180°. Two angles on a straight line are called **supplementary**. If one angle on a straight line is 120°, what is the other angle?

**Answer**: 60°

**Hints**:
- Hint 1 (free): "Supplementary angles add to 180°."
- Hint 2 (1⭐): "The whole straight line is 180°. One piece is 120°."
- Hint 3 (2⭐): "You know the total is 180° and one piece is 120°. What's left?"

**Teaching Point**: Supplementary angles sum to 180° (a straight line).

#### Level 4-1-5: Grogg's Angle Error ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Category**: Find the Error  
**Difficulty**: ⭐⭐⭐

**Story Context**:
> Grogg scratches his head. "I found a 60° angle. Its complement must be 120° because 60 + 120 = 180!"

**Problem**:
> What's wrong with Grogg's reasoning?

**Answer**: Grogg confused complementary (sum to 90°) with supplementary (sum to 180°). The complement of 60° is 30°, not 120°.

**Hints**:
- Hint 1 (free): "What's the difference between complementary and supplementary?"
- Hint 2 (1⭐): "Complementary angles add to 90°, not 180°."
- Hint 3 (2⭐): "60° + ? = 90°"

**Teaching Point**: Don't mix up complementary (90°) and supplementary (180°)!

#### Level 4-1-6: Triangle Angle Sum
**Type**: Teaching  
**Category**: Pattern Discovery  
**Difficulty**: ⭐⭐

**Story Context**:
> Polly Portions points to a triangular flag. "Every triangle has a special property about its angles!"

**Problem**:
> A triangle has angles of 50°, 60°, and 70°. Add them together. Now try with angles 30°, 90°, and 60°. What do you notice?

**Answer**: Both sum to 180°. The angles of ANY triangle always add to 180°.

**Hints**:
- Hint 1 (free): "Calculate each sum separately first."
- Hint 2 (1⭐): "50 + 60 + 70 = ? and 30 + 90 + 60 = ?"
- Hint 3 (2⭐): "Both equal 180. This works for ALL triangles."

**Teaching Point**: The three angles in any triangle always sum to exactly 180°.

#### Level 4-1-7: The Missing Angle
**Type**: Practice  
**Category**: Strategic Practice  
**Difficulty**: ⭐⭐

**Problem**:
> A triangle has two angles: 45° and 85°. What is the third angle?

**Answer**: 50°

**Hints**:
- Hint 1 (free): "Use the triangle angle sum property."
- Hint 2 (1⭐): "45 + 85 + ? = 180"
- Hint 3 (2⭐): "45 + 85 = 130. So 180 - 130 = ?"

**Teaching Point**: When you know two angles of a triangle, subtract their sum from 180° to find the third.

#### Level 4-1-8: Parallel Lines
**Type**: Teaching  
**Category**: Thinking  
**Difficulty**: ⭐⭐

**Story Context**:
> The temple has two walls that never meet, no matter how far they extend. "Those are parallel!" exclaims Lizzie.

**Problem**:
> Two lines are parallel if they never intersect, no matter how far you extend them. Look at the letter 'H'. Which lines in the letter H are parallel?

**Answer**: The two vertical lines (the sides of the H)

**Hints**:
- Hint 1 (free): "Which two lines in 'H' would never meet?"
- Hint 2 (1⭐): "The horizontal bar crosses both vertical lines, but what about the verticals?"
- Hint 3 (2⭐): "The two vertical lines run alongside each other—they'll never intersect."

**Teaching Point**: Parallel lines have the same direction and never cross.

#### Level 4-1-9: Perpendicular Paths ⭐
**Type**: Challenge  
**Category**: Thinking  
**Difficulty**: ⭐⭐⭐

**Story Context**:
> Captain Claw's map shows two paths meeting at exactly a right angle. "Those paths are perpendicular!"

**Problem**:
> Lines are perpendicular if they meet at a 90° angle. In a rectangle, how many pairs of perpendicular sides are there?

**Answer**: 4 pairs (each corner creates a pair of perpendicular sides)

**Hints**:
- Hint 1 (free): "How many corners does a rectangle have? Each corner is a 90° angle."
- Hint 2 (1⭐): "At each corner, two sides meet at 90°. That's one pair per corner."
- Hint 3 (2⭐): "4 corners × 1 pair each = 4 pairs of perpendicular sides."

**Teaching Point**: In a rectangle, every side is perpendicular to its two neighboring sides.

### Chapter 2: Multiplication Summit (Weeks 4-6)
**Topics**: Distributive Property, Multiplication Algorithm, Units Digit  
**BA Syllabus Weeks**: 3-5  
**Narrative**: Climb the Multiplication Summit where ancient mathematicians left clever shortcuts carved in stone.

#### Level 4-2-1: Breaking Apart Numbers
**Type**: Teaching  
**Category**: Multiple Solution Paths  
**Difficulty**: ⭐⭐

**Story Context**:
> Lizzie finds a multiplication shortcut on the temple wall: "To multiply 23 × 7, the ancients broke it into (20 + 3) × 7!"

**Problem**:
> Use the distributive property to calculate 23 × 7. Break 23 into 20 + 3 first.

**Answer**: 161

**Hints**:
- Hint 1 (free): "Distribute: (20 + 3) × 7 = 20×7 + 3×7"
- Hint 2 (1⭐): "20 × 7 = 140. Now find 3 × 7."
- Hint 3 (2⭐): "140 + 21 = ?"

**Teaching Point**: The distributive property lets us break difficult multiplications into easier pieces: a(b + c) = ab + ac.

---

#### Level 4-2-2: Clever Breaking
**Type**: Practice  
**Category**: Strategic Practice  
**Difficulty**: ⭐⭐

**Problem**:
> Calculate 99 × 6 by thinking of 99 as 100 - 1.

**Answer**: 594

**Hints**:
- Hint 1 (free): "Distribute: (100 - 1) × 6 = 100×6 - 1×6"
- Hint 2 (1⭐): "100 × 6 = 600. Now subtract 1 × 6."
- Hint 3 (2⭐): "You have 600 and need to subtract the small adjustment. What do you get?"

**Teaching Point**: Numbers near round numbers (like 99 near 100) are often easier to multiply using subtraction.

#### Level 4-2-3: Two Methods Compare
**Type**: Thinking  
**Category**: Multiple Solution Paths  
**Difficulty**: ⭐⭐⭐

**Story Context**:
> Grogg multiplies 15 × 12 by doing 15 × 10 + 15 × 2 = 150 + 30 = 180.  
> Lizzie multiplies by doing 12 × 15 = 12 × 10 + 12 × 5 = 120 + 60 = 180.

**Problem**:
> Both get 180! Which method do YOU prefer? Now use your preferred method for 25 × 14.

**Answer**: 350

**Hints**:
- Hint 1 (free): "Either breaking method works. Pick whichever seems easier."
- Hint 2 (1⭐): "Try: 25 × 14 = 25 × 10 + 25 × 4 = 250 + 100"
- Hint 3 (2⭐): "250 + 100 = 350"

**Teaching Point**: Multiple solution paths exist—choose the one that makes the numbers friendliest!

#### Level 4-2-4: The Units Digit Mystery

#### Level 4-2-5: Units Digit Pattern

**Category**: Pattern Discovery

**Problem**:
> What is the units digit of 7 × 7 × 7 × 7? (That's 7⁴)

**Answer**: 1

#### Level 4-2-8: Grogg's Calculation Error ⭐

#### Level 4-2-9: Units Digit Challenge ⭐
**Type**: Challenge

**Problem** (from BA4 Syllabus):

#### Level 4-3-1: Power Notation
**Type**: Teaching  
**Category**: Strategic Practice  
**Difficulty**: ⭐⭐

**Story Context**:
> Ancient mathematicians had a shorthand: instead of writing 5 × 5 × 5, they wrote 5³.

**Problem**:
> What does 2⁵ mean? Calculate its value.

**Hints**:

#### Level 4-4-2: The Multiplication Principle

#### Level 4-8-12: Why Does Equivalent Fractions Work? ⭐
**Type**: Thinking  
**Category**: Pattern Discovery  
**Difficulty**: ⭐⭐⭐

**Story Context**:
> Professor Owlbert poses a deep question: "We know that 2/3 = 4/6 = 6/9. But WHY does multiplying top and bottom by the same number give the same fraction?"

**Problem**:
> Explain why 2/3 = 6/9 using this idea: What happens if you take a pizza cut into 3 slices and cut each slice into 3 smaller pieces?

**Answer**: Cutting each of the 3 slices into 3 pieces gives 9 pieces total. The 2 original slices become 6 smaller pieces. You have the same amount of pizza — you just cut it into more pieces! Multiplying by 3/3 is the same as multiplying by 1.

**Hints**:
- Hint 1 (free): "If the pizza has 3 slices and you cut each into 3 pieces, how many total pieces?"
- Hint 2 (1⭐): "3 slices × 3 cuts each = 9 pieces. Your 2 slices become 2 × 3 = 6 pieces. Same pizza, more cuts!"
- Hint 3 (2⭐): "Multiplying top and bottom by the same number is like cutting each piece into smaller pieces. The AMOUNT doesn't change — only the number of pieces does. That's why n/n = 1."

**Teaching Point**: Multiplying by n/n is multiplying by 1. It changes how we COUNT the pieces, not how much we HAVE. This is the scaling principle of equivalent fractions.

#### Level 4-10-12: Fraction of a Fraction
**Type**: Thinking  
**Category**: Thinking  
**Difficulty**: ⭐⭐⭐

**Story Context**:

**Problem**:
> Without computing, which is larger: 2/3 of 4/5, or 4/5 itself? Explain your reasoning without multiplying.

**Answer**: 4/5 is larger. Taking 2/3 OF something always makes it smaller (since 2/3 < 1), so 2/3 of 4/5 must be less than 4/5.

**Hints**:
- Hint 1 (free): "When you take a fraction (less than 1) OF something, does the result get bigger or smaller?"

- Hint 3 (2⭐): "Since 2/3 < 1, we have 2/3 × 4/5 < 1 × 4/5 = 4/5."

**Teaching Point**: Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 SHRINKS the number. Multiplying by a fraction greater than 1 GROWS it. This is fundamental to fraction intuition!

#### Level 4-10-13: Grogg's Division Mistake ⭐
**Type**: Challenge

**Teaching Point**: Division by a fraction less than 1 makes numbers LARGER, not smaller. "How many small pieces fit in the big thing?" — more small pieces fit than big ones!
